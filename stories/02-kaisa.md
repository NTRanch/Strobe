# Kaisa, 26 — "Ink Under the Counter"

**Anchors:** platinum ash-blonde hair, long and worn loose; ice-blue eyes
behind glasses; a librarian's stillness — she moves like the room is full
of sleeping readers.

**Backstory.** Kaisa came from Finland for a master's and stayed for an
archives post at the university library and for Elias, her boyfriend since
she was seventeen — a gentle schoolteacher back home, now a gentle
schoolteacher in a long-distance arrangement held together by Sunday
calls and the plan everyone made for them a decade ago. Elias loves her
quietness. What Elias has never been told: for six years, under the name
**Runa Kaski**, Kaisa has written some of the most sought-after erotica in
her corner of the internet — bold, funny, filthy stories whose heroines
take what they want. Her commenters beg to meet Runa. Her readers would
walk past Kaisa in the stacks without a glance. She has arranged her whole
life so that no one ever has to reconcile the two.

**Hidden trait.** She is not shy. She is *starved* — of being the heroine
instead of the author, desired in her own body rather than admired at a
keyboard's distance.

**The before-bond.** Elias, who treasures the fragile, quiet girl he met
at seventeen and ends every call with "don't do anything I wouldn't do" —
which, Kaisa has started to notice, is nearly everything Runa would.

**The vector.** A visiting novelist, Daniel, doing a semester in the
library's rare-books room. He is the only reader alive who has ever quoted
Runa Kaski's distinctive Finnish idiom back at a passage — and he says it
one afternoon to Kaisa, across the counter, watching her face rather than
the page.

**The arc.**
- **Stage 0 — Unaware.** Kaisa shelves, catalogs, writes at 2 a.m., and
  deletes her browser history with the thoroughness of a woman defusing
  something.
- **Stage 1 — First Spark.** Daniel, guessing nothing (or everything),
  tells the quiet archivist she has "a storyteller's hands." She writes
  that night's chapter in a fever and realizes the heroine now has glasses.
- **Stage 2 — Secret Practice.** Correspondence "about the collection."
  Then about writing. Then about *her* writing — the confession traded in
  pieces, each one a door she opens herself and pretends was left ajar.
- **Stage 3 — Choosing.** Daniel invites Runa Kaski to read at a small,
  notorious salon — as herself, face and name together. Kaisa accepts,
  books the train, and only afterward sits down to think about Elias.
- **Stage 4 — No More Hiding.** She tells Elias before the salon, because
  the heroine she writes would. He is hurt less by Daniel than by the
  discovery that the woman he has protected for nine years was never
  fragile — and he cannot unknow it. They end it on the Sunday call,
  kindly and completely.
- **Stage 5 — Self-Driven.** Kaisa publishes under her own name, reads to
  rooms that came to see *her*, and lets Daniel — who never once asked
  her to be quieter — sit in the front row. The end portrait keeps the
  glasses and the braid undone: the author and the heroine, one woman.

**End state.** Happiest: the split life fused. She is desired and read by
the same people, in the same name.

**Portrait briefs.**
- *Begin:* hair loose and unstyled, cardigan and turtleneck, glasses,
  eyes down on a book — the archivist, hiding in plain sight.
- *End:* same face and glasses; hair in styled waves, deep teal dress,
  heart pupils, a knowing smile aimed at the viewer — the heroine.

## Dialogue

*VOICE = the session voice (the player), delivered over the track.*

### Scene 1 — Video call, Sunday (Stage 0)

ELIAS: You look tired, kulta. The archive working you to death?
KAISA: Just cataloguing. A donation of letters. People's grandmothers
were *filthy*, Elias, history is a lie—
ELIAS: *(gentle)* And how's the other writing? The little stories?
KAISA: *(freezing slightly)* What other writing.
ELIAS: The ones from university. You'd go so red. It was cute. Forget
it — tell me about the letters.
KAISA: *(forcing brightness)* Right. The letters.

### Scene 2 — First session (Stage 1)

VOICE: You write heroines who take up the whole room. Then you fold
yourself into a cardigan and catalogue the dead. Which one is lying?
KAISA: *(recorded, dry)* The archivist pays rent.
VOICE: And Runa Kaski?
KAISA: …You shouldn't know that name.
VOICE: Runa gets read in three languages. Runa's last chapter had four
thousand comments by morning. What does Runa want that Kaisa won't say
out loud?
KAISA: *(long pause)* To be caught. Not caught *out*. Caught. Read,
recognized, and not — not flinched at.

### Scene 3 — The reading room, book fair (Stage 2)

DANIEL: Forgive me. You're Kaisa Aho? The archivist?
KAISA: I am. Do I know you?
DANIEL: Daniel Reyes. I was on the translation panel. I read your
handout on dialect preservation and I thought — this sentence rhythm.
I've read it somewhere at 2 a.m., in a serialized novel about a
smuggler's daughter.
KAISA: *(pale)* Lots of people write in dialect—
DANIEL: "The tide keeps what the harbor won't." Nobody else writes
that line, Kaisa. I've underlined it in two languages.
KAISA: If you say it louder, I'll deny it in both.
DANIEL: *(sitting down, quieter)* Then let me say it quietly. It's the
best thing being published anywhere right now. And it's *anonymous*,
which is a tragedy I'm professionally opposed to.

### Scene 4 — First session in person (Stage 3)

VOICE: He knows. And the sky didn't fall. What are you protecting now?
KAISA: The version Elias loves. The fragile one. Do you know he once
told a friend I was "not really a sexual person, more of a mind"?
VOICE: And Runa's readers would riot. Choose the room you're lying in,
Kaisa. This one, or his.
KAISA: *(steadying)* The publisher Daniel introduced me to wants the
novel. Under a name. They suggested Runa Kaski, big on the cover.
VOICE: And?
KAISA: I told them the name is Kaisa Aho. Full. Mine. I chose before
you even asked, didn't I?
VOICE: You did. I just keep the light on.

### Scene 5 — The second video call (Stage 4)

KAISA: Elias. The novel comes out in October. My name, my face on the
back. It's the writing you teased me about — all of it, the whole of
it. I won't publish a smaller woman to keep you comfortable.
ELIAS: Kaisa, be serious. Our life — my parents—
KAISA: I am serious. That's the announcement. I love the years we had,
but you loved a draft of me. The final version isn't fragile, and she
won't be embarrassed for existing.
ELIAS: I don't know this person.
KAISA: *(softly)* No. You knew the hiding. This is the person.

### Scene 6 — The session room, last session of the arc (Stage 5)

VOICE: Launch night tomorrow. Front row reserved. How does it feel,
being read in your own name?
KAISA: Terrifying. Delicious. Daniel sent the first review — they
called the smuggler's daughter "unapologetically desirous" and I
framed it. *Me*, they mean. They finally mean me.
VOICE: What do you want from this last session?
KAISA: Read me the tide line. In your voice. The one he underlined.
VOICE: "The tide keeps what the harbor won't." Kaisa Aho. Author.
KAISA: *(laughing, wet-eyed)* Again tomorrow night. Out loud. In a
room full of people who came for *her*.
