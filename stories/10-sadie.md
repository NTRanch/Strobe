# Sadie, 28 — "Roses and Rigging"

**Anchors:** copper hair in a short messy bob; hazel-green eyes;
freckles; a tomboy's forward lean — she enters rooms like she's already
mid-sentence, usually about a winch.

**Backstory.** Sadie climbs towers for a living — a broadcast
transmission engineer who rigs antennas at three hundred feet and is,
by acclamation, one of the guys: beer league, group chat, the friend
every man she knows calls first when anything breaks. Her boyfriend
Mikey has been her best friend since trade school; they fell into
dating the way you fall into a couch — comfortably, years ago, and the
romance has the exact temperature of the friendship: warm, funny, and
utterly devoid of courtship. Mikey has never brought her flowers,
opened a door with intent, or looked at her the way men in old films
look at women, and Sadie tells the group chat she'd *die* of
embarrassment if anyone did. This is the biggest lie in her life. What
Sadie wants — what she has wanted since the one high-school dance where
a boy treated her like a girl for eleven minutes — is to be courted.
Dressed up. Desired *formally*, on purpose, as a woman and not a buddy.
She keeps a dress in the back of her closet with the tags on. It has
been there for two years.

**Hidden trait.** The wish to be courted and cherished as a woman —
old-fashioned, deliberate femininity, chosen by a tomboy who is tired of
pretending she'd die of it.

**The before-bond.** Mikey, her boyfriend, who says "you're not like
other girls" as the highest compliment he owns, and cannot hear the
sentence's wound.

**The vector.** Edmund Hartley, 36, owner of the ballroom studio whose
rooftop array Sadie maintains — a formal, unhurried man who thanks her
crew with written notes, asks permission before moving her toolkit, and
one day, finding her at height in the wind, says the sentence that
cracks her whole life open: "Miss Sadie, you have the hands of an
engineer and you should be *danced with* by someone who knows what a
privilege that is. May I have the honor, when you're next on the
ground?"

**The arc.**
- **Stage 0 — Unaware.** Sadie out-burps the group chat, services
  Edmund's array in coveralls, and goes home past the closet with the
  tagged dress without looking at it. Most nights.
- **Stage 1 — First Spark.** Night shifts wreck her sleep; she books
  strobe sessions. In trance the VOICE asks what she protects with all
  that noise, and out it comes, mortifying and true: "If I ever let
  anyone see me want to be *pretty*, I'll never live it down. So I've
  wanted it in the dark for ten years."
- **Stage 2 — Secret Practice.** She takes Edmund's offered dance lesson
  "to scope the studio's wiring," in boots. Then a second lesson. She
  wears the dress at home alone and practices being looked at by the
  mirror until she can hold its gaze. Mikey notices nothing; the
  mirror notices everything.
- **Stage 3 — Choosing.** Edmund invites her to the studio's winter
  ball — as his guest, formally, with a corsage he asks permission to
  send. Sadie stands in her closet holding the dress and the whole
  shaking truth of her wanting, and says yes out loud to the empty
  room before she texts it.
- **Stage 4 — No More Hiding.** She tells Mikey before the ball — the
  hardest conversation of her life, conducted in the truck they rebuilt
  together. Mikey is quiet a long time, then honest: "I love you like a
  brother, Sadie. I've just been too cowardly to say the dating part was
  habit." They keep the friendship — the real one — and bury the
  imitation.
- **Stage 5 — Self-Driven.** Sadie still climbs towers in coveralls and
  out-burps nobody anymore (she's lost interest), wears the dress —
  tags long gone — to every ball Edmund's studio holds, and glows under
  formal courtship like a lamp finally wired to its own switch. The end
  portrait: copper bob curled, emerald dress, a corsage at her wrist
  and heart pupils — laughing, not at a joke this time, just at being
  *seen*.

**End state.** Happiest: she gets both truths at once — the wrench and
the roses — and no longer has to amputate the second to keep the first
respected.

**Portrait briefs.**
- *Begin:* messy bob, work shirt and coveralls half-off the shoulder, a
  smudge of grease on one cheek, buddy-grin — one of the guys.
- *End:* same face and freckles; bob softly curled, emerald evening
  dress, wrist corsage, heart pupils, a shy-glowing smile — the woman
  being courted, at last.

## Dialogue

*VOICE = the session voice (the player), delivered over the track.*

### Scene 1 — The truck, after a climb (Stage 0)

MIKEY: You're not like other girls, Sadie. Joel's girlfriend took forty
minutes to pick a *hi-vis color*. You'd have been up the tower already.
SADIE: Damn right. Flowers die, small talk's a war crime, and I'd rather
be buried in coveralls. *(beat, quieter)* That's me. That's the brand.
MIKEY: *(laughing, driving)* The brand! Never change, Sadie.
SADIE: *(watching a florist's window pass)* …Never planning to.

### Scene 2 — First session (Stage 1)

VOICE: Let the beat sort the noise out. You carry a lot of loud, Sadie.
What is it protecting?
SADIE: Sleep hygiene, mostly. You said these sessions were for—
VOICE: What is it protecting?
SADIE: *(long silence)* …The dress. There's a dress in my closet, tags
on, two years. If anyone ever saw me want to be *pretty* — really want
it, flowers and dancing and being looked at like a *woman* — I'd never
live it down. So I've wanted it in the dark for ten years.
VOICE: Then let's want it in the light a little. One session at a time.

### Scene 3 — The ballroom studio, "wiring inspection" (Stage 2)

EDMUND: You move like the tower taught you balance and nobody taught
you it was *for* anything. One dance, Miss Sadie. Not flirtation —
instruction. Your boots are acceptable.
SADIE: This is a wiring scope. I am scoping the wiring.
EDMUND: Then scope it in waltz time. My hand at your back — may I?
SADIE: *(a breath)* …You ask. Nobody asks. They just— okay. Okay, yes.
*(beat)* You're going to ruin my whole brand, Edmund Hartley.
EDMUND: Your brand, Miss Sadie, is a coverall. You are not.

### Scene 4 — The closet (Stage 3)

*(Sadie, alone, holding a phone and a printed invitation.)*
SADIE: "The honor of your company at the Winter Ball. A corsage will be
sent, with your permission." Permission. He asks permission to send
flowers, Mikey. *(to the dress)* Two years you've hung there like an
accusation. *(pulling it off the hanger)* Fine. *Fine.* Yes. Yes to all
of it. God help me, I want to be looked at like that — and I'm going to
let it happen where people can see.

### Scene 5 — The truck, the last honest drive (Stage 4)

SADIE: Mikey. I need to say a thing and I need you not to make it a bit.
I love you. You've been my brother since I was nineteen. But we've been
playing couple for years out of habit, and meanwhile I have been
starving for a kind of wanting you've never felt for me — and shouldn't
have to fake.
MIKEY: *(long quiet)* …You know what the worst part is? I'm relieved.
I thought I was broken, not feeling it right. You're not broken either,
Sadie. We just built the wrong shape out of a true thing.
SADIE: Keep the truck on weekends?
MIKEY: Obviously. But I'm not helping you move the dress. That thing's
got two years of drama in it.

### Scene 6 — The session room, last session of the arc (Stage 5)

VOICE: Your heart rate settles in half the time it used to. What did the
year teach you?
SADIE: That "one of the guys" was a place I hid, not a place I lived.
Edmund sent roses to the *tower* last week — the whole crew saw, Voice,
and I didn't die. I stood at three hundred feet with a corsage on my
harness and I have never felt so much like myself in my life.
VOICE: And the dress?
SADIE: *(laughing, heart in her eyes)* Tags off. Worn twice. Third
dance is Friday — and this time I'm asking *him* to dance.
