# Cordelia, 27 — "What the Tide Wants"

**Anchors:** deep teal hair in long loose waves; aqua eyes; a serene
surface with weather underneath — she looks like calm water over a drop.

**Backstory.** Cordelia is a marine biologist at the city aquarium, the
woman who gives the gentle talks to school groups and knows every animal
by name and history. She is engaged to Graham, an actuary whose love is
a spreadsheet of care: insurance optimized, holidays booked a year out,
risk assessed and declined. Graham is afraid of the water — afraid of
most unquantifiable things — and Cordelia has been shrinking her oldest
self to fit inside his safety margins for five years. Because the truth
is: the woman who talks so softly to children free-dives alone at dawn,
swims out past the breakers in weather that closes beaches, and has
always, since girlhood, felt the pull she jokes about and means — the
siren's pull, outward and downward, toward the wild and the deep and the
arms of whoever is brave enough to swim *toward* the singing instead of
lashing himself to a mast.

**Hidden trait.** Wildness: risk, depth, and the desire to lure and be
chased — to be wanted by someone who hears the song and swims out
anyway, eyes open.

**The before-bond.** Graham, her fiancé, whose idea of a beach day is a
risk assessment with a view.

**The vector.** Jules Marchetti, 30, a free-diving instructor and
tall-ship sailor wintering in her harbor town, who first sees her at
dawn — a teal-haired figure walking out of black water like something
the sea decided to return — and, instead of asking if she's all right,
says: "The current turns at six. Tomorrow I'll race you to the buoy.
Loser buys coffee. Winner names the next depth."

**The arc.**
- **Stage 0 — Unaware.** Cordelia gives her talks, files her research,
  agrees the cove is "too rough this weekend, you're right," and swims
  alone at dawn in secret like an affair with the ocean itself.
- **Stage 1 — First Spark.** She books strobe sessions to train breath
  control for longer dives. In trance, under the slow count, the VOICE
  asks what the deep gives her that land doesn't, and she answers
  instantly: "It doesn't want me safe. It wants me *real*."
- **Stage 2 — Secret Practice.** Dawn races with Jules become the fixed
  star of her week. He never once tells her to be careful; he tells her
  to be *honest* — about the depth, the cold, the wanting. She starts
  lying to Graham about where the mornings go, and hates the lying more
  than she fears the truth.
- **Stage 3 — Choosing.** Jules invites her to crew a night crossing —
  three days, open water, real weather, the full wild of it. She stands
  in her kitchen looking at Graham's printed itinerary for their
  honeymoon (a resort with a lifeguarded lagoon) and books the crossing
  instead of booking the resort's dive package. Her hands don't shake.
  That steadiness is the answer.
- **Stage 4 — No More Hiding.** She tells Graham everything over the
  kitchen table — the dawn swims, Jules, the crossing — not as apology
  but as geography: "You love the shore of me, Graham. He swims out.
  I've been drowning politely in two feet of water for five years, and
  I won't anymore." Graham grieves the safe woman he invented; the
  engagement ends in tears and, finally, honesty on both sides.
- **Stage 5 — Self-Driven.** Cordelia leads the aquarium's new
  open-water program, swims where she likes with a partner who races
  her to every buoy, and wears her wildness in daylight. The end
  portrait: teal hair wet-look and wild, shimmering dress, heart
  pupils — the siren, singing in the open, happier than the shore ever
  made her.

**End state.** Happiest: no longer loved at the shoreline. The song was
never a danger to be managed; it was the woman herself, and she found
the swimmer who knew it.

**Portrait briefs.**
- *Begin:* hair neatly parted, modest sundress and cardigan, serene
  distant smile — the educator, safe and shoreline-bound.
- *End:* same face; waves wild and wet-look, shimmering scale-pattern
  evening dress, heart pupils, a siren's confidence — the deep, calling.

## Dialogue

*VOICE = the session voice (the player), delivered over the track.*

### Scene 1 — The kitchen, Sunday (Stage 0)

GRAHAM: I've re-run the cove. Rip current risk is moderate, and the
jellyfish advisory— honestly, Cordelia, the pool at the hotel is heated
and it's *gorgeous*. Why risk the ocean?
CORDELIA: You're right. The pool is lovely.
GRAHAM: See? This is why we work. We assess, we choose the smart option.
*(kissing her cheek)* Early night? Dawn patrol for your samples?
CORDELIA: *(alone, setting a 4:40 alarm labeled "samples")* Samples.
Yes. That's what we'll call them.

### Scene 2 — First session (Stage 1)

VOICE: Slow count now. In for four… hold for four. You're training the
breath for the deep, Cordelia. What does the deep give you that the land
doesn't?
CORDELIA: *(without hesitation)* It doesn't want me safe. It wants me
real. Down there I don't have to be small, or gentle, or *assessed*.
VOICE: And on land?
CORDELIA: *(a soft, sad laugh)* On land I'm engaged to the assessment.

### Scene 3 — The harbor, dawn (Stage 2)

JULES: You cut twenty seconds off your buoy time. You're not a
biologist who swims, Cordelia. You're a swimmer who does biology to
explain herself.
CORDELIA: And what are you?
JULES: The man who hears singing past the breakers and swims *out*.
Everyone else ties themselves to the mast. I never understood the
appeal of surviving you.
CORDELIA: *(quietly, wrecked and elated)* Careful. The tide takes men
who talk like that.
JULES: Then let it take me honestly.

### Scene 4 — The kitchen, the itinerary (Stage 3)

GRAHAM: *(on the phone)* —the resort confirmed, lagoon side, and I've
declined the reef excursion, too exposed. We're going to have the safest
honeymoon in history. *(pause)* Cordelia? You there?
CORDELIA: *(looking at Jules's message: "Night crossing. Three days.
Real weather. Say yes and mean it.")* I'm here. I need to tell you
something about the honeymoon. And about the dawns. And about what
"samples" means.
GRAHAM: …What does "samples" mean, Cordelia?

### Scene 5 — The kitchen, the truth (Stage 4)

CORDELIA: It means I've been swimming out past the breakers alone for
five years rather than tell you the woman you proposed to doesn't exist
at the shoreline. You love the shore of me, Graham. Jules swims out. I
met the whole ocean in myself this year, and I can't unmeet it.
GRAHAM: I kept you *safe*.
CORDELIA: You kept me *shallow*. It felt like drowning in two feet of
water. I'm done drowning politely — I'd rather risk the deep and
actually live there.

### Scene 6 — The session room, last session of the arc (Stage 5)

VOICE: Your breath holds are up another forty seconds. What changed?
CORDELIA: I don't come here to escape my life anymore, Voice. I come to
*tune* it. I race him to the buoy at dawn and lose on purpose some
days, just for the chasing. *(laughing)* The school groups asked why I
glow now. I told them: vitamin sea.
VOICE: And the song?
CORDELIA: I sing it in daylight now. That's all the song ever wanted.
