# Vivienne, 28 — "The Voice in the Walls"

**Anchors:** black-cherry hair in long waves; violet eyes; a singer's posture —
chin high, breath low, every entrance an arrival.

**Backstory.** Vivienne sang. Not as a hobby — she was two seasons into a
real lyric-soprano career when a nodule scare at 24 ended it, or so she let
everyone believe. The truth she has never said aloud: the doctors cleared
her in six weeks. She quit anyway, because the opera world wanted her voice
*correct* — and what she loved, what she still dreams about, was never
correctness. It was the moment a full house goes silent because *she* has
decided it will. Being watched. Being wanted by a room. She now teaches
voice to wealthy amateurs, is engaged to Marc — a kind, tone-deaf arts
administrator who adores her the way one adores a painting: from a careful
distance, without ever touching the canvas.

**Hidden trait.** Exhibitionist hunger. She does not miss singing; she
misses *being beheld* — desired, openly, by people who came to desire her.

**The before-bond.** Marc, her fiancé. A good man who loves the respectable
instructor and would be quietly horrified by the woman who used to take a
third curtain call in a state she can only describe as feral.

**The vector.** A late-night cabaret two streets from the conservatoire,
run by a pianist named Odette who heard Vivienne hum one night and simply
said: "Friday. Midnight. Wear something honest." Odette does not want her
correct. Odette wants her *seen*.

**The arc.**
- **Stage 0 — Unaware.** Vivienne corrects other women's breathing for a
  living and hums only in the shower, where the tiles can't applaud.
- **Stage 1 — First Spark.** She sings one song at the cabaret, "as a
  favor," in a borrowed dress. The room's silence before the applause does
  something to her that Marc's devotion never has. She walks home shaking.
- **Stage 2 — Secret Practice.** Friday midnights become a liturgy. A
  stage name. A dress that lives in a locker, not her closet. She tells
  herself it is vocal maintenance. She practices entrances.
- **Stage 3 — Choosing.** Offered a regular Saturday set — real money, a
  real billing — she takes it *knowing* Marc's circle will find out
  eventually, and finds she has stopped hoping they won't.
- **Stage 4 — No More Hiding.** Marc sees the billing poster. The fight is
  quiet and devastating: he asks her to give it up, and she hears herself
  say no with a steadiness that surprises them both. The engagement ends
  without cruelty. He keeps the instructor. The cabaret keeps her.
- **Stage 5 — Self-Driven.** Vivienne headlines. She programs her own
  sets, wears what she wants, takes the silence before the applause like a
  sacrament. In the end portrait she is mid-laugh, gown off one shoulder,
  looking straight at the viewer the way she once only dreamed a room
  would look at her.

**End state.** Happiest: a woman whose desire to be beheld is no longer a
shameful leak in a respectable life but the organizing principle of a
better one.

**Portrait briefs.**
- *Begin:* hair in a neat low bun, high-necked navy dress, pearl stud,
  reserved half-smile — the instructor, composed and untouchable.
- *End:* same face; hair down in full waves, off-shoulder magenta gown,
  stage makeup, heart pupils, radiant and openly inviting — the headliner.

## Dialogue

*VOICE = the session voice (the player), delivered over the track.*

### Scene 1 — The recital hall, after the student showcase (Stage 0)

MARC: You were wonderful with them. The little one — Sofia? — she
actually projected to the balcony.
VIVIENNE: She has the instrument. She just has to stop apologizing for
its size.
MARC: *(laughing)* Listen to you. Tough as nails. Come on — donors'
reception. Smile like a brochure.
VIVIENNE: A brochure.
MARC: You know what I mean. My elegant fiancée. *(beat)* You never sing
at these things anymore. I miss it.
VIVIENNE: *(too quickly)* People's evenings are short, Marc.
MARC: Right. Right. Coat?

### Scene 2 — First session (Stage 1)

VOICE: You spend your life teaching other voices to fill rooms. Whose
voice fills yours?
VIVIENNE: *(recorded, startled)* I — nobody's. I'm the teacher. That's
the arrangement.
VOICE: Then why did your shoulders drop just now?
VIVIENNE: …Because nobody has asked me that in nine years. Ask it again.
VOICE: Whose voice fills yours, Vivienne?
VIVIENNE: *(barely audible)* After the wedding recitals stopped, I used
to stand in the empty hall and sing one aria. Just to hear a room hold
me. I told myself it was practice.
VOICE: It was a hunger. Hungers don't apologize. Say what it wants.
VIVIENNE: It wants… a room that stays. An audience that came for *me*.

### Scene 3 — The Halo Room, closing set (Stage 2)

ODETTE: You came back. Third Friday.
VIVIENNE: I told myself I was studying your left hand.
ODETTE: My left hand is flattered. And the rest of you?
VIVIENNE: *(a breath)* When you played under that student's aria last
week, the whole room turned. Not to her. To *us*. I have wanted that
turn my entire adult life and I have been calling it vanity.
ODETTE: Vanity is wanting it for nothing. You want it for the song.
There's a difference, and the difference is why you'll be on that stage
by spring.
VIVIENNE: Odette. I'm engaged.
ODETTE: I know, chérie. The stage is patient. Are you?

### Scene 4 — The dressing room mirror (Stage 3)

VOICE: You keep the gown in a garment bag behind your teaching coats.
Describe putting it on.
VIVIENNE: It's magenta. It's absurd. Marc would call it "a lot."
VOICE: And when it's on?
VIVIENNE: I stand taller. My voice sits lower. I look like the woman
the arias were written about and I have spent nine years being the
woman who *explains* them.
VOICE: Who is she asking permission from?
VIVIENNE: *(long pause)* A man who loves me at half volume. …I'm going
to sing at the Halo Room's spring auditions. I chose it. It's chosen.

### Scene 5 — The apartment, honesty (Stage 4)

VIVIENNE: I need to tell you something true, and I need you to hear the
whole of it before you answer. I am not unhappy because you are cruel.
You are kind. I am unhappy because I am disappearing at a volume you
find comfortable.
MARC: Vivienne—
VIVIENNE: I sing in empty halls, Marc. I have a gown hidden like a
crime. I'm going to sing at the Halo Room in May, in it, and I can't
marry a man I'm hiding my own voice from.
MARC: *(quietly)* I always knew there was a bigger version. I think I
was just… hoping she'd stay theoretical.
VIVIENNE: She can't. I'm sorry. She really can't.

### Scene 6 — The session room, last session of the arc (Stage 5)

VOICE: Headliner in May. Full house booked out in a week. And tonight?
VIVIENNE: Tonight my name is above the door and my fiancé — my *former*
fiancé — sent flowers with a card that said "be loud." I cried at a
card, Voice.
VOICE: What do you want from this last session?
VIVIENNE: Nothing hidden. Walk me out there. In the voice. I want to
hear you say my name the way the room will.
VOICE: Vivienne. The room is full. Every eye is yours. Sing.
VIVIENNE: *(laughing, shining)* I intend to. I finally, finally intend
to.
