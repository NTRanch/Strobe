# Maren, 30 — "Lay Your Burden Down"

**Anchors:** chestnut hair in a practical ponytail; green eyes; capable
hands, tired eyes, a nurse's economy of movement — nothing wasted, nothing
soft.

**Backstory.** Maren has been the responsible one since she was nine:
mother's illness, younger brothers, nursing school on night shifts. She
is now the ER nurse every charge nurse prays for — the one who takes the
crashing patient, the furious family, the new grad crying in the supply
room. She is engaged to Jonah, a surgical resident, a good and efficient
man who loves her competence the way generals love logistics. Their
relationship runs like a well-staffed unit: meal prep, shared calendar,
sex on Sundays if neither is post-call. Nobody has ever asked Maren what
she needs. She would not know how to answer. What she dreams about —
literally, in the sleep she barely gets — is a room where someone else
decides everything, where her only job is to be kept.

**Hidden trait.** Surrender. Not weakness — the opposite appetite: to lay
the burden down and be cared for with the same totality she gives.

**The before-bond.** Jonah, her fiancé, who introduces her as "the most
capable person I know" and has never once seen her cry.

**The vector.** Dr. Adrian Voss, 41, the anesthesiologist whose unshakeable
calm runs every trauma bay he enters — the one man in the hospital who
gives orders *to* Maren, notices when her hands shake, and says "sit"
the way other people say "please."

**The arc.**
- **Stage 0 — Unaware.** Maren holds the department, the family group
  chat, and the wedding planning together, and falls asleep mid-sentence
  on the couch most nights.
- **Stage 1 — First Spark.** After a brutal shift she books a strobe
  session for sleep. In trance, asked what she wants, she hears herself
  say: "For someone to tell me to stop." She cries in the session room
  and tells no one.
- **Stage 2 — Secret Practice.** Weekly sessions become the only hour of
  her life where she decides nothing. At work she starts seeking Voss's
  bays. At home she practices saying "I can't tonight" and feels guilty
  for hours.
- **Stage 3 — Choosing.** Voss, finding her asleep upright in a call
  room, covers her with a blanket and leaves a note: "Dinner. Thursday.
  You choose nothing; I have it." She reads it six times. She goes.
- **Stage 4 — No More Hiding.** She tells Jonah the truth — not about
  Voss first, but about the exhaustion and the dream underneath it. Jonah
  is bewildered, then honest: he does not want a woman who needs keeping;
  he wants a partner who runs the line beside him. The engagement ends
  with real sorrow and no lies.
- **Stage 5 — Self-Driven.** Maren moves to a sane schedule, keeps two
  evenings a week that belong entirely to being cared for, and — the end
  portrait — lets her hair down, literally and in every other way,
  glowing with the rested radiance of a woman who finally receives.

**End state.** Happiest: the caretaker, kept. Her strength was never the
problem; being the only one allowed to have needs was.

**Portrait briefs.**
- *Begin:* ponytail, scrubs, stethoscope, polite exhausted smile — the
  nurse everyone leans on.
- *End:* same face; hair down, soft glam, black evening dress, heart
  pupils, a surrendered, rested smile — the woman who is leaned *toward*.

## Dialogue

*VOICE = the session voice (the player), delivered over the track.*

### Scene 1 — The apartment, 11 p.m. (Stage 0)

JONAH: You're asleep sitting up again.
MAREN: I'm resting my eyes. Did you eat? There's chicken in the—
JONAH: I ate at the hospital. You have the venue call at nine tomorrow,
and your brother's car thing—
MAREN: Handled. Both. Go to bed, Jonah, you're post-call.
JONAH: You're amazing. I don't know how you do it.
MAREN: *(to herself, moving his shoes to the rack)* Neither do I.

### Scene 2 — First session (Stage 1)

VOICE: Follow the pulse. Four counts in… and let the shoulders answer.
What do you need, Maren?
MAREN: Sleep. Just— sleep.
VOICE: Beneath the sleep. The beat will hold you. What do you *need*?
MAREN: *(long pause)* For someone… to tell me to stop. Please. Just—
someone whose job it is to stop me.
VOICE: Then stop. That's the whole instruction. Stop.
MAREN: *(crying, laughing a little)* I don't know how.
VOICE: I know. That's why you came to me.

### Scene 3 — The trauma bay, weeks later (Stage 2)

VOSS: Nurse. You've been on your feet nine hours.
MAREN: We're short, Doctor, I can—
VOSS: That wasn't information I requested. Sit. Drink this. That's a
medical order, if it helps you obey it.
MAREN: *(sitting, stunned)* …It does, actually.
VOSS: I know. *(beat)* I see you, Maren. Even when you're hiding in
competence. Especially then.

### Scene 4 — The call room (Stage 3)

*(Maren finds the note again, reads it aloud to the empty room.)*
MAREN: "Dinner. Thursday. You choose nothing; I have it." *(pause)* One
dinner. That's all this is. One dinner where I don't have to— *(she picks
up her phone, types: "Thursday. Yes.")* —decide.

### Scene 5 — The kitchen, honesty (Stage 4)

JONAH: Is there someone else?
MAREN: There is a *me* else, Jonah. That's the honest answer. I'm so tired
that I dream about being looked after like a patient. And you don't want
that woman. You want the one who runs the line.
JONAH: …I do. God help me, I do.
MAREN: Then we have to stop. Because I've met her — the other one — and
I like her too much to send her back.

### Scene 6 — The session room, last session of the arc (Stage 5)

VOICE: Same pulse as the first night. What's different?
MAREN: I sleep now. All night. Someone cooks and I let him. Someone
decides and I let him. *(smiling)* And at work I'm *better*, Voice. Being
kept didn't make me smaller. It made me possible.
VOICE: And what do you need?
MAREN: Nothing. *(beat)* Isn't that a miracle? Nothing at all.
